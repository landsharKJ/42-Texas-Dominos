42Texas dominoes

How this table plays

Four players, two partnerships. Partners sit across from each other. First team to the marks target wins.

Counting

Each hand is worth 42 points: 1 per trick, plus the count dominoes. 5-5 and 6-4 are worth 10 each. 5-0, 4-1 and 3-2 are worth 5 each.

Reading the table

Under each team on the score card you can open the tricks it has won this hand. Each trick lists the dominoes in the order they were played, the winning domino is outlined in gold, and count dominoes (worth 5 or 10) have a gold underline. The list clears when the hand ends.

Bidding

Starting left of the dealer, everyone gets one turn to bid or pass. Bids run from 30 to 41 points, then 42 (1 mark) and 84 (2 marks). A mark bid means your team must win every trick. Make your bid and your team scores 1 mark (or the marks bid). Get set and the other team scores them. The hand stops as soon as the result is decided.

Suits and trump

  • The domino led sets the suit: its higher end. A double sets its own number. A led 3-2 is a lead of threes.
  • To follow, play any domino with that number on either end. When threes are led, 3-6, 3-5 and 1-3 are all threes.
  • Inside a suit the double is highest, then dominoes rank by their other end. With threes led, 3-3 beats 3-6, which beats 3-5, and so on down to 3-0.
  • A domino carrying the trump number is a trump and follows nothing else. The double of the trump number is the top trump, then the rest rank by their other end.
  • Trump beats everything else. If no trump is played, the highest domino of the led suit wins.
  • Doubles as trump: all seven doubles form the trump suit, 6-6 highest.
  • Follow me: no trump. The higher end of the led domino sets the suit, and both ends can follow it.

Colors

The Colors button changes how the table looks for you only: domino color, colored pips (each number gets its own color), table color, the color of the rail around the table and your rack, and a high-contrast mode with bold outlines and shapes as well as colors for the two teams. Nobody else sees your choices.

Learning aids

The Cheat sheet button (top right) opens a one-page reminder of how counting works, how suits follow, and the suit names, with a small counting drill. While you play, three more aids can help: a suggested domino with the reason for it, a plain-English explanation after every trick, and small badges showing which of your dominoes are worth count points. Like bid suggestions, they use only your own dominoes and what has already been played. You can hide them for yourself at any time, and the host can turn them off for the whole table in the lobby.

Bid suggestions

When it’s your turn to bid, a suggested bid appears with its estimated chance of success, and the suggested button is outlined in gold. It is worked out only from your own dominoes and the bids made so far, never from anyone else’s hand. You can hide it for yourself at any time, and the host can turn it off for the whole table in the lobby.

Specials

  • Nello: bid 1 or 2 marks, and lose every trick. Your partner sits out, so each trick has three dominoes. When you win the bid you choose how doubles count: as their own suit, as the highest of their number, or as the lowest of their number.
  • Sevens: bid 1 or 2 marks, and win every trick. No following suit; the domino whose total is closest to 7 wins, and ties go to the first one played.
  • Splash: with 3 or more doubles, bid 2 or 3 marks. Your partner calls trump and leads. Win every trick.
  • Plunge: with 4 or more doubles, bid 4 or 5 marks. Your partner calls trump and leads. Win every trick.

The dealer is forced to bid 30 if everyone else passes, unless the host turns that off. A bid has to beat the current high bid, so a 1-mark Nello can’t outbid a 1-mark Sevens.
